The first title

Shams al-Maʿārif al-Kubrā

This work, composed by the Berber scholar Aḥmad ibn ʿAlī al-Būnī, who passed away in Cairo in around 1225 and was a contemporary of Ibn ʿArabī, stands as the seminal text in Islamic letter-mysticism. It deals with the study of Arabic letters, magic squares, and the attributes of the divine Names.

For eight centuries it passed through Ottoman archives, West African religious schools and the Mughal imperial court, always reproduced by hand. Earlier English translations covered only fragments and selected passages. The complete text had never been published in English, until this edition.

The complete set spans 1,443 pages in two volumes and includes all four sections of the longer recension, accompanied by a translator's preface examining al-Būnī's biography and the manuscript history, along with a comprehensive glossary of specialised terminology.
